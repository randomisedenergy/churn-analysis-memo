Where early churn comes from, and what to do about it

Cohort-based churn analysis and leadership memo | Dev Sanghrajka | SQL, Python, financial modelling

This is a case study on a synthetic dataset: 1,140 customers who signed up between January and December 2025, generated with Claude for practice. The figures illustrate the method. They are not from a real company.

Recommendation

Launch a targeted 90-day retention program for customers who sign up on a monthly plan through paid channels. This one segment loses about half its customers in the first 90 days and drives roughly two-thirds of all early churn.

If the program cuts that segment's early churn by 30%, it would protect about $141K in annual revenue. Test it with an 8-week split pilot before committing.

What the data shows

I measured 90-day churn for the 855 customers who signed up from January to September, so every one of them has a full 90-day window.

  • Monthly / Paid is 194 of 855 customers (23%) but about 95 of the 145 customers lost in the first 90 days (about 65%).
  • Annual plans barely churn early, at 2.5% to 3.3%, whatever the channel.
  • On monthly plans, paid sign-ups churn 2.6 times as often as organic ones.

Retention stays between 78% and 87% in every cohort, so nothing is getting worse over time. A product-wide fix would be spread too thin. A fix aimed at one segment pays back much faster.

The proposed intervention

  • A guided onboarding sequence in the first 14 days, focused on reaching the product's key first-use moment.
  • A check-in at day 30 and day 60 for customers whose usage drops.
  • An offer to move to an annual plan at a discount, since annual customers churn at under 4%.

Limits of this estimate. The 30% reduction is an assumption, not a measured result, and the pilot exists to test it. The model assumes each retained customer stays a full 12 months, which is optimistic. It also leaves out the cost of the program and any discount on annual plans. The yearly sign-up volume is a scenario input; a different volume scales the figure up or down proportionally.

Next step

Run an 8-week pilot on new Monthly / Paid sign-ups, split 50/50 between the program and the current experience. Track 90-day churn, the share who move to annual plans, and revenue per customer net of any discount. Scale the program only if early churn falls by at least 20%.

Method and SQL

I generated the dataset in Python, loaded it into SQLite and ran the two queries below. The dataset and queries are saved alongside this page as customers.csv and queries.sql.

-- 90-day churn by segment
SELECT plan||' / '||channel AS segment, COUNT(*) AS customers,
  ROUND(100.0*SUM(CASE WHEN months_to_churn<=3 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)/COUNT(*),1) AS churn_90d_pct,
  ROUND(AVG(mrr_usd),0) AS avg_mrr
FROM customers WHERE signup_month<='2025-09'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY churn_90d_pct DESC;

-- 90-day retention by signup cohort
SELECT signup_month, COUNT(*) AS customers,
  ROUND(100.0*SUM(CASE WHEN months_to_churn IS NULL OR months_to_churn>3
    THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)/COUNT(*),1) AS retained_90d_pct
FROM customers WHERE signup_month<='2025-09'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
